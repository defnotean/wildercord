# Resonant Strikes

A blade opens a seam; a spell answers it. Land **elemental spell damage and an Aura strike on the same living foe within 1.2 seconds** to trigger a resonant strike. Either can land first. You can do this yourself, or coordinate with an allied player.

The swordsman must have at least Glow, Aura enabled, an eligible weapon in hand, and **four Aura** available. A full, coated ordinary swing counts, as do an Aura slash and the first answering hit of an art or written technique. Glancing swings and secondary chain sparks do not prime it.

## Read the meeting

The blade's school shapes the incision. The spell's material unfolds through it over three short beats. There are no casting circles around this reaction, and its shapes stay at the struck foe. Complementary schools add their own material details.

| Spell element | Reaction | Complementary blade | What it gives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fire | Ember Shear | Gale: Bellows Cut, flame caught in airflow | A little stance pressure |
| Frost | Rime Splinter | Ember: Thermal Cleave, venting steam | Brief Slowness I |
| Storm | Forked Edge | Stone: Grounding Stroke, a floor fracture | Moderate stance pressure |
| Wind | Crosswind | Hourglass: Borrowed Step, crossing wakes | Two seconds of Speed I for the swordsman |
| Earth | Faultline | Rime: Gravel Rime, frost in the fracture | Stronger stance pressure |
| Life | Greenwake | Crimson: Grafted Edge, red sap in unfolding leaves | Up to one health restored to the swordsman against creatures |
| Void | Hollow Seam | Starlit: Veiled Prism, broken glints at a closing seam | Two seconds of Weakness I on creatures |
| Arcane | Prism Cut | Hourglass: Second Hand, separated prism facets | A short Exposed mark for a follow-up spell |
| Time | Echo Beat | Gale: Slipstream Echo, displaced cuts receding | Brief Resistance I for the swordsman |
| Blood | Red Thread | Hollow: Hollow Pulse, a dark incision beneath taut threads | Extra stance pressure against wounded creatures |

The complementary names describe material variations within these ten reaction families. Other known spell/school pairings work too, including matching elements. A fused spell contributes the element of its first successful damaging component on that foe.

## Coordinate safely

The swordsman pays **four Aura per reaction**. Each swordsman and each target must then rest **four seconds** before another resonance. Switching targets or weapons does not reset the swordsman's rest; a reconnect does not erase its remaining cooldown. Pending hit openings expire and do not survive a server restart.

Each spell payment can prime a particular target only once. Repeated beams, storms, echoes and linked hits from that payment cannot keep priming it after the rest expires. A fresh paid cast can. This helps coordinated timing matter more than the number of damage ticks.

The reaction adds **25% of the weaker hit's actual health damage**, capped at **three health points against creatures** and **0.75 against players**, before normal defenses. Server damage scales can reduce that bonus; they cannot raise it above these ceilings. The extra damage meets spell shields, armor, boss spell resistance, Warding and the cast guard. It produces no extra rune triggers or hit-resource rewards.

Player slows last twelve ticks, player stance pressure is capped, and the Life heal, Void weakness and Blood wound utility do not apply against players. Frost never freezes the player in place. Wind and Time offer a short response window to the swordsman.

Two players count as partners when they are teammates, or cannot harm one another under the server's PvP rules. Duel opponents are never partners. Both must still be allowed to harm the foe, alive in the same dimension, and within thirty-two blocks of it. Protected pets, teammates, sparring partners, misses, shielded spells, immune hits and killing blows cannot prime a reaction.

Training dummies report the damage they take before restoring themselves, so they can be used to practise the timing. The Life reaction cannot restore health from a dummy or the Practice Room. Pure healing and control spells without health damage do not prime this damage-timing system. Existing elemental Aura marks and ordinary spell reactions remain separate systems.

## A field note: two hands

The Marchkeepers' early records speak of *two hands at a seam*. One carried a sword, the other a Cord. A swordsman learned to wait for the wet crack of frost; a mage learned to hear an edge catch stone. Their finest meetings were brief. The foe had time to turn, and the partners had to find one another's cadence again.

That tradition survives beside the village's Three Bows. The stewards welcome a mage to practise outside the arena, but a tournament bout still forbids spells touching its rival.
