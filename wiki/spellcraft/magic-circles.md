# Magic Circles
{: .no_toc }

Every spell writes its own magic circle, and the circle is made of the spell's runes. Learn to read it and
you know what a spell does before it lands: yours, another player's, or the one a Runebound is about to throw
at you.

## Where you'll see circles

The same circle, built from the same runes, appears everywhere a spell does:

| Where | What it is |
|---|---|
| Under your feet when you cast | A flat circle about two blocks across, for a moment |
| Behind your shoulders while you charge | A full circle follows your rear plane; a smaller forward focus forms the chosen delivery |
| In a Runebound's or a boss's hand | Its **telegraph**: the spell it's about to cast (see [below](#telegraphs)) |
| On the ground under a Domain | The Domain's floor is the spell's own circle, as wide as the Domain |
| In front of someone with a Shield | The Shield's circles are the circle of the spell that raised it (see [Shields]({{ '/spellcraft/shields/' | relative_url }})) |
| On a block holding a glyph | A faint copy of the stored spell's circle (see [Imbuing]({{ '/spellcraft/imbuing/' | relative_url }})) |
| Beside the Cord screen | The spell you're editing, opening again whenever you change it |
| Beside the Grimoire page | The secret spells you've found, one after another |

A circle is **never bigger for a longer spell**. More runes mean more points on its star and smaller roundels
on them, never a bigger circle.

## The parts of a circle

<img src="{{ '/assets/images/spell-circle.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="A charging magic circle: a heavy frame with rays, a band of script, a patterned band, a seven-pointed star with a coloured roundel on each point, inner rings and a seal in the middle" class="shot">
<span class="caption">A seven-rune spell: seven star points, seven roundels, the script band round the edge and the seal in the middle.</span>

A circle is built like a classic magic circle, from the outside in:

1. **The frame:** a heavy ring and a fine one just inside it, with short **rays** sticking out on the star's
   points. Count the rays and you've counted the star's points.
2. **The script band:** a band of writing just inside the frame. The "letters" are the **emblems of the
   spell's runes**, in casting order, written round and round the circle.
3. **The pattern band:** a narrower band in the **ring pattern of the spell's first effect**. Its motif tells
   you the spell's element at a glance, even from a distance.
4. **The star:** a star polygon with **a point for every rune**. Spells of one to three runes get a six-pointed
   star and four runes an eight-pointed one, with the roundels spread round it; from five runes up, the star
   has exactly one point per rune (five points for five runes, seven for seven).
5. **The roundels:** on the star's points, a small circle for **each rune**: that rune's own ring pattern
   around its emblem, in the rune's colour. They're in **casting order, starting at the top** and going round
   (clockwise, as the Cord screen draws it).
6. **The inner rings:** two fine rings round the middle.
7. **The seal:** in the centre, the **emblem of the spell's first rune**, which is usually its shape. If the
   spell starts with an effect (cast on yourself), the seal is that effect's emblem.

The circle is drawn in the colour of the **spell's first effect**, and teal when there's no effect to take a
colour from. (The circle under a caster's feet goes by the first group, so a spell whose first shape carries
no effect of its own casts a teal one.) A charging circle for a spell with no effect takes the colour of your
[leaning]({{ '/spellcraft/casting/' | relative_url }}#leaning), or gold if you have none.

### How a circle moves

A circle **opens in stages**: the frame first, then the script and the pattern bands write themselves in, the
star's lines draw from point to point, the seal settles in the middle, and last the roundels appear **one by
one in casting order**. A charging circle opens as fast as the charge builds, so you can watch a caster's
spell being spelled out. Once open, the bands turn slowly in opposite directions, the star turns more slowly
still, and the whole circle breathes a little. It fades out at the end.

## Reading a rune's ring and emblem

Every rune has its **own ring pattern** and its **own emblem**, and no two runes share either. That's what
makes a circle readable: once you know a rune's ring or emblem, you'll spot it in any circle. Even without
knowing them all, the rules below tell you a lot.

<img src="{{ '/assets/images/a-rune-rings.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Eight runes' rings, each with its emblem in the middle: Bolt's double teal line and square emblem, Fire's solid orange ring with flame teeth and a round emblem, Amplify's dashed gold ring and diamond emblem, On Hit's violet chain and octagonal emblem, Frost's ring of crystals, and three fused runes whose rings are braids of two coloured strands and whose emblems are split down the middle" class="shot">
<span class="caption">Rings and emblems drawn flat. Top: one rune of each family. Bottom: a Frost ring, then three fused runes, each a braid of its two elements with a split emblem.</span>

### The line says the family

| Family | The ring's line | The emblem's frame |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | A **double** line | A **square** |
| Effect | A **solid** line | A **circle** |
| Modifier | A **dashed** line | A **diamond** |
| Link | A **chain** of linked ovals | An **octagon** |

### The motif says the element

Along an effect's ring runs a motif of its element (on the outside, the inside, both or alternating, and with
small marks of its own, so that each rune differs):

| Element | Colour | Motifs on its rings | The glyph inside its emblems |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fire | orange | flame teeth, flames, twin prongs, embers | a peak like a flame |
| Frost | pale blue | diamonds, crystals, snowflakes, shards | a snowflake |
| Storm | yellow | lightning bolts, chevrons, forks, sparks | a lightning bolt |
| Wind | pale green | waves, swirls, arches, feathers | two wavy lines |
| Earth | brown | blocks, battlements, steps, pillars | a downward wedge |
| Life | green | buds, leaves, sprouts, berries | a flower on its stem |
| Void | violet | crescents, eyes, hollows, rifts | a crescent |
| Arcane | pink | stars, crosses, triangles, rune marks | a four-pointed star |
| Time | pale gold | hourglasses, dials, falling sand, loops | an hourglass |
| Blood | crimson | drops, fangs, veins, hearts | a drop |

Shapes' rings carry arrows, frames, triangles or spokes; modifiers' dots, crosses, carets or notches; links'
hooks, keys, knots or wards. Their emblems use a small set of plain signs (arrows, crosses, bars, lozenges)
inside their family's frame.

Colours follow the runes: **shapes are teal, modifiers gold, links violet**, and every effect wears its
element's colour. So a teal square seal and a ring of violet chain on one point tell you "a shape, and a link"
before you've read a single emblem.

### Fused runes

A [fused rune]({{ '/runes/fused/' | relative_url }}) is made from two elements at the Fusion Altar, and its
ring and emblem wear **both**:

- Its **ring is a braid** of two strands crossing twice on every stretch of the ring, each strand in one
  element's colour. Its own element's motif runs along the outside and its partner element's along the inside.
- Its **emblem is split down the middle**: its own element's glyph on the left, its partner's on the right,
  each half in its element's colour, with a small seam above and below.
- A fusion of an element **with itself** (Conflagration is Fire and Fire, Singularity Void and Void) shows
  that element's glyph widened across both halves, its second half in a lighter shade of the same colour.

A fused rune counts as an element of its own (Firestorm, Fire and Wind, counts as Fire), and that's the
colour its roundel and the rest of the circle take when it's the spell's first effect.

### Knots and secret spells

- A [Knot]({{ '/fusion-altar/knots/' | relative_url }}) takes one point on the star like any rune, but wears
  the **plain link ring and emblem**, whatever is tied inside it. A Knot hides its spell.
- A [secret spell]({{ '/spellcraft/secret-spells/' | relative_url }}) has a circle no ordinary spell can make:
  in place of the star and roundels, a **centrepiece of its own**, with the seal still in the middle. Its
  script band still spells out its runes, for anyone who can read them.

## A worked reading

Take the circle in the picture at the top of this page:

- **Seven rays** on the frame and a **seven-pointed star**: seven runes.
- The **seal** is a teal square emblem: a shape comes first. It's **Nova**.
- The **pattern band** carries fire motifs, and the circle is orange: the first effect is Fire's.
- Reading the **roundels** round from the top: **Nova** (teal, square), **Flashfire** (orange, round),
  **Widen** (gold, diamond, a dashed ring), **Shock** (yellow, round, a ring of lightning), **Amplify** (gold,
  diamond), **On Hit** (violet, octagon, a chain) and **Burst** (teal, square).

So the spell is `Nova · Flashfire · Widen · Shock · Amplify · On Hit · Burst`: a nova of fire and lightning
round the caster, the Flashfire widened (it has a radius, so Widen finds it before the Nova) and the Shock
amplified. After On Hit comes a Burst, but nothing after it, so that burst would go off wherever the nova hit
and do nothing: the Cord screen would warn "Burst has no effect after it". Reading a circle tells you a
spell's weak spots too. The script band round the edge repeats the same seven emblems.

A quicker way to read a circle in a hurry:

1. **Colour** tells you the element (orange: fire; pale blue: frost; yellow: storm).
2. **The seal** is the first rune, usually the shape: where it's going. (A spell that starts with an effect is
   cast on its caster.)
3. **Count the roundels** for how long the spell is: a long spell usually does more than one thing.
4. **Look for violet chain rings** among the roundels: a link means something happens later (on hit, after a
   delay, when hurt).

## Telegraphs

Monsters that cast show you what's coming. A [Runebound]({{ '/world/runebound/' | relative_url }}) about to
cast:

- holds out its spell's **circle in its right hand** (kept to the side, so its face stays in view) for about
  **a second** before the spell goes off,
- lights up its **nameplate**: the rune icons and spell name it always shows turn bold and white, between
  **» «** marks,
- makes its **rune marks** flare, burning toward white, with sparks streaming off them,
- and, for a **Zone, Rain, Mine or Domain**, marks the ground where the spell will land.

You'll also hear it: an evoker's hiss of preparation and a ringing chime.

Dungeon bosses and the Archivist telegraph the same way, for a little longer (about a second and a half), and
their **boss bar** names the spell being cast ("The Archivist · casting Shock Rain").

When you see a telegraph, you have time to act:

- **Step out** of a marked patch of ground.
- **Shoot the spell down**: a bolt of yours that meets an enemy's bolt in the air bursts with it (see
  [Playing Together]({{ '/social/playing-together/' | relative_url }})).
- **Raise a Shield** at the last moment to **parry** the spell back at its caster (see
  [Shields and parrying]({{ '/spellcraft/shields/' | relative_url }})).
- **Read it**: a frost circle means you should keep fire ready for a Shatter; a violet chain means something
  more is coming after the first hit.

## Other circles

Many spells draw circles of their own as they work: a Zone's ornate ring on the ground for as long as it
lasts, the ring a charged spell's reticle draws, a Rain's circle opening in the sky, the seals of lingering
effects like a Sandstorm or a Vortex, the circle that turns under you while you attune a Blank Rune, and the
circle that turns over the Fusion Altar as it works. They're drawn in the same thin lines of light, but only a
spell's own circle, with its star and roundels, spells out its runes.

## Choose a mechanism and a tradeoff

Twelve [circle disciplines]({{ '/spellcraft/circle-disciplines/' | relative_url }}) give their own iris, petals, turbine, lattice, basins, furnace, braid, compass, eye, crescents, storm forks or eclipse. Plain shapes also select these mechanisms for presentation, without a gameplay bonus.
